## Executive Summary  

| Pillar | Asymptotic Cost (baseline) | Critical Bottleneck ( \(D\!\to\!10^{6}\) ) | Condition‑Number \( \kappa\) Limit (≈ failure) | Zero‑Copy / Concurrency | Recommended “next‑gen” Upgrade |
|--------|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------------|
| 1. Cayley‑Stiefel matrix‑free retraction | \(\mathcal{O}(DK^{2}+K^{3})\) (Sherman‑Morrison‑Woodbury) | **Memory‑bandwidth** for the \(D\times K\) “tangent” matrix; cache‑misses dominate beyond \(D\approx 2\!\times\!10^{5}\) | \(\kappa\lesssim 10^{5}\) (SMW blow‑up) → **catastrophic loss of orthogonality** for \(\kappa>10^{6}\) | Requires a *copy‑free* view of the \(D\times K\) factor; multi‑threaded GEMM with NUMA‑aware placement is essential. | (a) Block‑wise *hierarchical* SMW with adaptive regularisation; (b) Randomised sketch‑based retraction (e.g. Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform) to reduce \(D\)‑dependence to \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(K^{2})\); (c) Mixed‑precision (FP16‑accumulate‑FP64) with iterative refinement. |
| 2. Clifford \(Cl(D)\) bivector rotors (decoupled 2‑D planes) | \(\mathcal{O}(D)\) per rotor (pairwise plane rotations) | **Synchronization latency** when aggregating \(D/2\) independent plane updates; the “global” rotor product becomes a reduction over \(10^{6}\) terms. | \(\kappa\) of the underlying metric (e.g. Gram matrix) > \(10^{6}\) → **loss of backward stability**; Higham bound \(\mathcal{O}(\kappa\epsilon_{\text{mach}})\) exceeds \(10^{-9}\) for double precision. | Zero‑copy is natural (rotor stored as packed bivector); however, *in‑place* updates across threads cause false sharing unless padded. | (a) Use *Householder‑style* reflectors on the Clifford algebra to collapse the \(D/2\) planes into \(\mathcal{O}(\log D)\) depth via butterfly networks; (b) Adopt *scaled* Clifford basis (norm‑preserving) to keep \(\|R\|\approx1\) and bound \(\kappa\); (c) Employ *Krylov‑subspace* exponential‑integrator for the bivector generator, giving higher‑order backward stability. |
| 3. Order‑5 Padé‑Taylor Polar iteration (QDWH fallback) | \(\mathcal{O}(D^{2})\) dense matrix‑matrix multiplies per iteration (dominant term) | **Latency of global reductions** for the scalar coefficients (trace, Frobenius norm) – each iteration incurs an \(\mathcal{O}(D)\) all‑reduce that becomes the *critical path* on WAN‑scale clusters. | For \(\kappa>10^{6}\) the Padé rational function loses monotonicity → iteration stalls after 3–4 steps; fallback to QDWH (cubic convergence) is required. | Zero‑copy is possible only when the matrix is stored in *column‑major* layout shared with the BLAS kernel; otherwise a copy is forced by the Padé coefficient computation. | (a) Replace the order‑5 Padé with a *variable‑order* Padé (order 7–9) selected by a cheap condition‑number estimator; (b) Use *communication‑avoiding* QR (CAQR) to compute the polar factors with \(\mathcal{O}(\log P)\) messages; (c) Mixed‑precision *iterative refinement* (FP16 → FP64) reduces bandwidth by > 2× while preserving \(\kappa\) tolerance up to \(10^{8}\). |
| 4. PMTP WAN Phase 10/11 – RaptorQ (RFC 6330) 4‑way cross‑block interleaving | \(\mathcal{O}(N)\) encoding/decoding, where \(N\) is the number of source symbols; per‑symbol cost is linear in the degree of the LT code. | **Network‑stack latency**: 4‑way cross‑block interleaving forces a *pipeline* of four independent UDP streams; the slowest stream dictates overall latency, especially under asymmetric loss. | Effective \(\kappa\) of the erasure channel (ratio of lost to received symbols) > \(10^{6}\) → decoding matrix becomes ill‑conditioned; Gaussian elimination on the dense parity‑check matrix blows up.
